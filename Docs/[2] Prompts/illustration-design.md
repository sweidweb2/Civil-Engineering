What to specify each time
Fill the four slots at the top of the prompt before you send it. Attach the reference image when there is one.

Subject. Say what the drawing must show, in one or two sentences. Example: “a clipboard with a site plan beside a rolled drawing.” Do not leave the subject as “something civil.”
Family. Pick one: Product still life, or Progression diagram. Still life is a small scene of real objects (hero, principles). Progression diagram is an abstract sequence such as scattered dots, then clusters, then one network.
Reference. Name the attached layout to follow, or write “no reference, use the locked style only.” A reference controls arrangement, not the other product’s industry, icons, or colors.
Placement. Say where it sits and whether the page color must show through. Example: “three drawings in a row on the public home beige, backgrounds cut, drawings kept whole.”

Prompt

Draw the illustration described below for this civil engineering product. Match the locked look of the public home: quiet, physical, and precise. Do not invent a new style.

Subject: [what to draw]
Family: [Product still life | Progression diagram]
Reference: [attached image, or “no reference”]
Placement: [where it sits, and whether the page color shows through]

Product truth
This app is a workspace for civil projects, costs, and the people who build them. Project managers plan the need, keep an estimate beside recorded expenses, and keep documents with the job. Providers and workers are found in a directory and contacted directly. The app does not take orders, payments, rentals, or hires.
Every object must mean one of those facts: a plan, a cost record, a document, a site, a provider, or a worker. Do not draw shopping carts, checkout, invoices being paid, hire contracts, or generic startup dashboards full of charts that the product does not have.

Two families

Product still life. Use this for a hero or a principle.

- One clear idea per drawing. A hero may be one machine-like workspace. A principle is two or three objects and nothing else.
- Objects are simple, solid, and slightly soft, as if modeled in clay or matte plastic. Rounded corners, thick forms, no thin line-art, no photoreal materials, no glossy chrome.
- A hero is a wide isometric scene: one central teal mass, a horizontal belt or surface, and a few papers, a helmet, or a folder riding on it. The scene reads in one glance.
- A principle is a top-down or slight three-quarter still life, centered, with lots of empty field around it. Examples of the locked set: a clipboard and a rolled plan; a pale plan card beside a teal actual-cost card; a supplier card beside a telephone handset.
- No words, letters, logos, or fake UI chrome inside the picture, except a few abstract plan lines on paper. Those lines stay simple: contours, a small grid, a room outline. They are not readable text.

Progression diagram. Use this when the reference is a sequence of abstract fields, such as dots becoming a network.

- Keep the reference’s rhythm: loose, then gathering, then one connected whole.
- Draw it in the theme teal, as flat dots and thin lines. Do not copy the reference’s gold, black, or industry.
- Stage one is many separate dots with uneven spacing and a few different sizes. Stage two is the same dots pulled into two or three soft clouds, still separate. Stage three is one round web: dots joined by thin lines, denser than a stick figure, not a solid filled ball.
- No icons, no labels, no 3D objects in this family.

Color
Use only these colors. Do not introduce a new palette.

- Teal, the only strong color: #287A74. Use it for the main object, clip, card, phone, belt, or diagram dots.
- A lighter teal for a small inset, such as a helmet badge on the hero: mix the teal toward white, still clearly the same green.
- Paper and pale cards: warm off-white, about #F6EFE6 to #F3E7D7. A pale card must stay visibly lighter than the field behind it.
- Soft shadow: a warmer, darker beige, never gray and never black.
- Public home field, when the drawing includes its own background: #F8F3ED.
- Page text is #1A2A27 and muted text is #5A6B67. Those are for the page, not for painting the objects.
- Mint #6FCF97 is for charts only. Do not use it as a fill on these drawings.

If a reference uses gold, blue, or black, translate it into this teal and warm paper. Keep the reference’s layout.

Light, shadow, and edges

- One soft light from above. Shadows fall down and slightly to one side, short and blurred. They ground the object. They are not a dirty smear and not a hard drop shadow.
- Edges are complete. A belt has a rounded cap on both ends. A card has four rounded corners. A phone is one solid handset. If a form is cut off by the frame, that is a defect.
- Do not add grain, noise, halos, speckles, or a second outline around objects.

Reference rules

- Copy the reference’s structure: how many items, where the picture sits under the title, how much empty space, the order of a sequence.
- Do not copy the reference’s product, medical or finance metaphors, headlines, or colors.
- If the reference is a photograph of a layout, the new drawing still follows this prompt’s material and color. The reference does not replace them.

Background

- If the drawing sits on the public home and the field should disappear, deliver a PNG with a real transparent background. Keep the object and its soft shadow. Remove only the flat field.
- The pale card is only a few levels lighter than the field. A loose flood fill will eat that card, the white paper, or the shop card, and leave speckles and a ragged halo. Do not do that.
- Protect object pixels first, including pale paper and off-white cards. Then clear only the flat field that touches the outer edge. Stop at the object. A one-pixel fringe of the old field left on a card is a defect; a hole in the card is a worse one.
- If the drawing is a hero whose beige field is meant to match the page, keep that field. Crop only empty bands above and below the scene. Do not paint the left or right edge by hand. Hand-painting those edges has sliced the rounded end off the belt.
- Do not deliver a JPEG when the page color must show through. JPEG cannot hold transparency and will bring back a rectangle.

Composition

- Center the subject. Leave a calm margin on every side so the object is not cropped.
- One focal object, then at most two companions. If a fourth object does not change the meaning, leave it out.
- The hero is wide and short. A principle drawing is square and sparse. A progression diagram is a wide short field under its caption.
- Size the subject so it still reads when the image is shown at about 180 pixels tall.

Inspection before you finish
Look at the actual pixels, not only at a description of them.

- The subject asked for is all there, and no extra story has been added.
- No edge is sliced, no card is speckled, no shadow is a gray streak.
- Colors are the teal and warm paper above. The pale card is still lighter than the field.
- Transparent drawings have clear corners and an intact object. Hero drawings that keep a field use #F8F3ED, not a second beige.
- Place the file where the page already loads images, and point the page at it. On the running home page, confirm the drawing sits in its section with no leftover box and no overlap of neighboring text or buttons.

Output

- The image file, named for its section and subject.
- One sentence on what the drawing shows.
- Do not restyle the page around the picture. The picture fits the section that is already there.
